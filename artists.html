Artist · 01

Raymond Loyola

Oil on Canvas — Figurative / Landscape

Raymond Loyola's practice is defined by a sustained attention to light — specifically, the quality of natural light across the Philippine landscape, and what happens to memory when light is rendered with precision on canvas.

His paintings are not illustrations of place. They are arguments about duration — about how long a particular quality of light lingers in the mind before it becomes something else. The horizon line in his work sits with a kind of authority: always just below center, where the land refuses to perform for the viewer.

Loyola's landscapes reward repeated looking. The more time spent with a single canvas, the more the surface reveals — layers of decision-making that are invisible at speed but become legible through slow attention.

"I am not trying to capture the landscape. I am trying to understand what it asked of me while I was standing in front of it."

Education

MFA, University of the Philippines College of Fine Arts, Diliman

Selected Exhibitions

Light Studies, Ten Contemporare, 2023
Impulses, Ten Contemporare, 2025
Montage (Group), Ten Contemporare, 2026

Artist · 02

Summer Pasana

Mixed Media — Spatial / Textural

Summer Pasana came to visual art from interior design, and the spatial intelligence that training gave her is entirely visible in her work. She approaches the flat surface the way an architect approaches a room — every plane is a decision about where the eye should rest, and where it should be refused.

Her mixed media practice draws on materials that carry their own history: raw linen, wax, tissue paper, sand collected from specific beaches. The surface of a Pasana work is a record of process, but not an expressive one. It is considered. She spends months on a single piece, building up and removing until the work contains exactly what it should.

Her work resists quick reading. It offers more to the viewer who is willing to stand still in front of it.

"I want the surface to do what a good room does — make you aware of the quality of your own attention."

Education

BFA Interior Design, De La Salle–College of Saint Benilde
Graduate studies, Rietveld Academie, Amsterdam

Selected Exhibitions

Surface / Depth (Solo), Ten Contemporare, 2023
Spaces and Stories, Ten Contemporare, 2025
Montage (Group), Ten Contemporare, 2026

Artist · 03

Jonet Carpio

Oil on Canvas — Figurative / Historical

Jonet Carpio's paintings are saturated with reference — literary, historical, mythological — but they do not announce this. The surface appears approachable until you look longer, and then the density of what is embedded in a single canvas becomes apparent.

He works in oil, slowly. His figurative practice places the human body in situations that carry the weight of narrative without illustrating it. You understand that something has happened, or is about to happen, without being told what it is.

His references include Baroque Spanish painting, Philippine revolutionary history, and the literature of José Rizal. None of this is cited explicitly. It is metabolised into image.

"The painting should contain more than the viewer first sees. If you get everything in thirty seconds, I have failed."

Education

BFA Painting, University of Santo Tomas
Residency, Ateneo Art Gallery, 2019

Selected Exhibitions

Verses, Ten Contemporare, 2024
Horizons In Between, Ten Contemporare, 2024
Montage (Group), Ten Contemporare, 2026

Gallery Statement

Our exhibitions are small. Our roster is selective. Every artist we represent is chosen because their work says something that cannot be said otherwise. We do not add artists to the roster because of market momentum or institutional endorsement. We add them because the work demands it.

If you are interested in an artist's work and want to understand more about their practice or available works, begin with an inquiry. We take the same care with collectors at every stage.
